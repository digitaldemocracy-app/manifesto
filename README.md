# Digital Democracy
## A Manifesto for the Voice of All

We elect people to speak for us.
And, far too often, they stop listening.

Not for lack of democracy. For lack of tools.

The democracy we have was designed in a time when the people's voice had to travel by horse. We chose someone, handed them our voice, and waited — years — to learn whether it had arrived. Today, each of us carries in a pocket the power to speak to the entire world. And still we wait.

Between the will of the people and the decision, a void has opened. In it live the lobby, self-interest, convenient silence. We accuse no one: we accuse the distance.

We want to close it.

## What we propose

A new kind of democracy. It begins by adding to the one that exists. How far it goes is not ours to decide — it belongs to each people. And that is exactly what we propose.

In practice, a platform: a single digital system, open and global, where anyone, anywhere in the world, can propose, debate and vote — and see, on the same screen, what their neighborhood, their country and humanity are deciding.

Built in public from the first line of code, it can be adopted by any people in their country, their state, their city, if they so choose. No one is required to join. And, added up, people by people, it lets humanity do what it has never done: organize itself to decide, together, what belongs to all.

On this platform:

**Everything is visible.** Proposals, arguments, counts, rules, and the code that runs them are public and permanent. Nothing is erased in silence.

**Your vote is yours.** Secret, always. And verifiable: you can confirm it was counted. You are free to tell the world how you voted — but no one, ever, can force you to prove it. So those who wish to stand in the open may; those who need protection have it.

**Each person exists only once, and counts exactly the same.** One identity, one vote. No bots, no manufactured crowds, no voices that weigh more because they shout louder or pay more.

**Every decision belongs to those it affects.** The neighborhood decides the neighborhood. The city decides the city. The country decides the country. And what belongs to all — the climate, the oceans, the pandemics — we all decide. Not a world government: a world that learns to decide together, each layer in its place. Because the lines on a map divide territories, not brothers and sisters.

**Debating is not fighting.** Here arguments have sides, and sides have arguments. The best-reasoned idea wins, not the most repeated.

**We choose the most capable to carry out what we decide together.** Those who plan and execute are chosen by the people, in broad daylight, for what they have done and what they know how to do. Competence is transparent; the power to decide remains equal for all.

**Some things are not put to a vote.** The dignity, the freedom, and the fundamental rights of every person stay out of the ballot box. The majority chooses the path; it does not choose who may walk it.

## To the institutions

We are not a party. We have no candidates. We replace no election, no parliament, no court.

We are an instrument: a faithful, permanent, verifiable portrait of what people think and want — available to those who govern, those who legislate, and those who judge. No government is required to listen to us. But those who do will govern with greater legitimacy, because they will know, before deciding, what their people would decide.

Anyone elected, from any party, may publicly commit to deciding as their people decide here — and everyone will be able to verify whether they kept their word.

We have not come to take anyone's place. We have come to give the institutions back what gave them birth: the voice of those who sustain them.

## Why now

Because the technology that took our attention can give back our voice. Because others have already begun — in Porto Alegre, in Taiwan, in Barcelona — and proved it is possible. And because no one has yet opened the doors to the whole world, at the same time, with everything in plain sight.

## An invitation

If you have ever felt your voice get lost along the way, it has a place here.

This manifesto belongs to no one: it is a starting point. Correct it, translate it, improve it. It is open, like everything that will come after.

Read. Disagree. Propose. Sign.

And let us, together, organize the world's democracy.

— Murilo Elias
Florianópolis, November 2026
