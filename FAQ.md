# Questions and objections

**Is this a world government?**
No. The opposite. Every decision is made at the level where it has effect, and no global body on the platform can override a local one. The global level exists only for what has no borders: climate, oceans, pandemics, the platform's own rules. "How far this goes belongs to each people" is written in the Manifesto on purpose.

**Are you a political party? Will you run candidates?**
No, and no. We have no candidates and endorse none. Any elected person, from any party, may publicly commit to deciding as their people decide here, and everyone will be able to check whether they did. We work with every party and belong to none.

**Which side are you on?**
The side of the process. The platform's rules are the same for every proposal, every argument, and every person. If you can show that a rule favors one side, that is a bug, and we will fix it in public.

**Who is funding this?**
Today: nobody. It is a personal project, built in the founder's free time and paid out of his own pocket. To build the platform we will need money, and when it comes everything will be open and instant: every account, every deposit, where it came from, how much and when, published the moment it happens. The founder's commitments are in [NEUTRALITY.md](NEUTRALITY.md).

**Why does the founder sign with his real name?**
Because asking for your trust while hiding would be a contradiction. The founder is not the point, and the Manifesto says so: it belongs to no one and is a starting point. He signs so that someone is accountable for it, and he states which side he is on and where the money comes from in [NEUTRALITY.md](NEUTRALITY.md).

**If everything is public, is my vote public?**
No. Your vote is secret. Always. You can verify it was counted. You may tell anyone how you voted, but the system never produces proof, so no one can force you to show it. The process is public; the ballot is yours. (Principle 2.)

**Why not simply make all votes public? Wouldn't that be more transparent?**
Public votes can be bought and coerced: an employer, a landlord, or a local boss can demand to see them. Every attempt at public online voting has run into this. Secrecy with verifiability gives you transparency of the result without exposure of the person.

**What about the right to be forgotten? Doesn't a permanent ledger violate data protection law?**
The ledger records events, not people's personal data. Your identity is verified once and kept separately from your public activity. What is permanent is the public record of the process: proposals, arguments, counts. We are designing with GDPR and LGPD from the start, and our legal reasoning will be published with the code.

**Why these ten languages?**
They are the ten most spoken languages in the world by total speakers (Ethnologue 2025). It is a rule, not a choice. Any other language can be added by volunteers; translations are open contributions.

**Why Russian? Do you intend to operate in Russia?**
We translate for people, not for governments. Russian is spoken by hundreds of millions of people in many countries. The same rule picked all ten languages.

**Isn't this just another participation platform? Barcelona, Taiwan, and others already have them.**
They proved it works, and we say so. What none of them did: a single open platform that any people can adopt, in layers from neighborhood to world, independent of any government or party, with a permanent public ledger and a secret, verifiable vote.

**What stops a majority from voting against a minority?**
Principle 7: fundamental rights are not put to a vote. The Universal Declaration of Human Rights is the reference. People can debate anything; they cannot vote away someone else's rights.

**Experts get a badge. Doesn't that give them more power?**
No. The badge shows verified expertise so that arguments can be weighed; it adds nothing to a vote. One person, one vote, always.

**How do you stop bots and fake accounts?**
Participation grows with verification. Anyone can read; an email lets you debate; a verified identity is required to vote with full weight. Counts are shown separately by verification level, so no one has to take our word for it.

**Can my country, city, or community just use this?**
Yes, when the platform exists. The code is open; the rules are public; adoption is voluntary. No one is required to join, and no one has to ask permission.

**Is any of this legally binding?**
Not by itself. Laws are made by the institutions each country has. The platform produces a verifiable record of what people want; what institutions do with it is up to them, and up to the people who elect them.

**How can I help?**
Read the Manifesto. Disagree with it in an issue. Translate it or review a translation. Sign it. And when the platform opens, bring one decision that matters where you live.
